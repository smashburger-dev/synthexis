# Recording protocol

The student models learn from recordings of Noa. Every segment of a recording shows one known instruction, so its labels come from the script, not from a labeler. SAM 3.1 adds the pixel masks afterwards.

Machine-readable script: [`spec/recording-protocol.json`](../spec/recording-protocol.json), generated by [`tools/make_protocol.py`](../tools/make_protocol.py). The recorder app plays it: it shows the current instruction large on screen with a countdown and the next instruction, records the camera, and writes the exact start and end of every segment in the movie's own timeline.

## Sessions

| Session | Camera | Light and look | When | Protocol | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | built-in FaceTime camera | usual studio light | Oct 27 | full, 10.7 min | train |
| S2 | built-in | different light (daylight or ceiling light), different shirt | Oct 27 | full, 10.7 min | train |
| S3 | iPhone via Continuity Camera, different angle (higher or from the side) | studio light | Oct 27 or 28 | full, 10.7 min | train |
| S4 | built-in | other day, other shirt, other part of the room in the background | Oct 28 | short, 6.3 min | held-out eval only, never trained on |

About 32 minutes of training video. At 30 fps that is about 58,000 frames; we label every second frame.

Light matters for speed, not only for quality: the first camera test at night delivered 18.6 fps with about 100 ms capture latency, because the built-in camera lengthens exposure in dim light. Record in good light so the camera holds 30 fps.

## What one session contains (full protocol)

| Block | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| A finger states | 12 finger states per hand (fist, thumb, index, thumb+index, index+middle, three, four, open, pinky, shaka, rock, ...), 6 s each, right hand then left hand, plus 6 two-hand combinations. Keep the pose and move the hand through the frame: near, far, rotated. | 3.0 min |
| B sweeps | slow fist-to-open and spread cycles in time with the on-screen pulse (4 s per cycle), counting 1 to 5 and back | 1.7 min |
| C face | neutral, mouth open sweep and hold, blink, wink left/right, eyes closed, smile, head yaw/pitch/roll sweeps, head turned with mouth moving (the occlusion case) | 1.7 min |
| D body | arms up and down, lean, step back with the torso in frame | 0.6 min |
| E objects | mug, phone, pen or drumstick, ball or fruit, one demo object: hold, rotate, move, put down, pick up, leave the frame | 1.7 min |
| F hard cases | hand in front of the face, overlapping hands, fast moves, hand at the frame edge, backlight, dim light | 0.8 min |

The short protocol (S4) keeps blocks A (4 s per state), C, E with three objects and F.

## Rules while recording

- Distance 40 to 120 cm from the camera, the way you sit at the desk while producing.
- When the script says one hand, the other hand leaves the frame. Labels mark it as absent.
- Jewellery, sleeves and clothes as you normally wear them in the studio.
- If you mess up a segment, keep going. Press the "mark bad" key and the recorder flags that segment; it is dropped from training.
- The first 0.7 s and last 0.3 s of every segment are not used as labels (you are still changing pose).

## Where the files go

`synthexis/data/recordings/<date>-<session>/`, for example `data/recordings/2026-10-27-S1/`:

- `cam0.mov` (and `cam1.mov` if a second camera records at the same time)
- `segments.json`: segment id, labels, start and end time in the movie, bad flags
- `session.json`: camera model, resolution, fps, light, notes

`data/` is git-ignored. Recordings stay on this Mac and in a private Modal volume used for labeling; they never go into the repository.

## After recording

1. Frames are extracted at 15 fps and uploaded to the Modal volume.
2. SAM 3.1 (open weights, Modal H100) segments the concepts person, face, eye, mouth, hand and the object names from block E. Estimated cost for all training sessions: under $10 (probe A: $0.051 per 1000 frames per concept, lower bound).
3. Apple Vision computes hand keypoints offline as targets for the keypoint head.
4. Segment labels from `segments.json` become finger-state and face-action targets.
5. Training and the eval gate run as described in [`plan.md`](plan.md).
