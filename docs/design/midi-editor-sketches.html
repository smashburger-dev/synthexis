What each finger plays: three sketches

Mock-ups of the synthexis window at its fixed width (1000 × 720 pt) in Noa's layout of 11-04: the camera fills the top third edge to edge, the top bar is frosted and fades out on its own, the chat line sits right under the camera, and everything else folds out below it over a heavily frosted copy of the camera frame. Colour only on values, amber for the left hand and cyan for the right. The three sketches differ only in the open "What fingers play" section. Steps are clickable, so the click count of each sketch can be tried. Example set-up in all three: right hand plays drum patterns on FPC (channel 3), left hand plays chords in A minor on Serum 2 (channel 1).

Idea. The hands are the menu. Each fingertip on the live skeleton carries what it plays: a 16-step ring for a pattern, a chord symbol for a chord. Shapes (peace, rock, any mask) sit as chips under the hand. A click on a fingertip opens its lane in the panel; templates fill it in one click, the Fill number sets an even spread of hits (Euclid) and every step stays clickable.

Fast because the thing you show the camera and the thing you click are the same picture, and the ring lights up when the finger fires, so Noa sees why a note played.

Weak spot. Only one lane is open at a time; comparing two fingers' rhythms means switching.

Idea. One row per action, 16 steps per bar, the way FL's channel rack shows a beat. Pattern rows hold steps; chord rows hold one chord per beat (a progression that advances while held) or a single chord. The row head names the finger and the FL channel; a row's ⋯ opens a piano roll for rows with several pitches.

Fast because everything is visible at once and FL users read it without learning anything new.

Weak spot. It is a table, not the hand: the link between a finger and its row is a label, and ten rows take most of the space under the chat line.

Idea. Each right-hand finger owns a step ring (the ring-knob language turned into a loop): drag outward for more hits, drag around to shift them, click single dots. The left hand gets a wheel of the seven chords in the key; fingers are chips you drop on a wedge, so a chord can never leave the scale. "Capture next bar" records what you tap (mouse or keys) into the selected ring, quantized.

Fast because two drags make a usable rhythm and one drop makes a chord; no step counting.

Weak spot. Rings hide exact positions past 16 steps; longer clips (2 to 8 bars) need the lane from A or B anyway.

My pick: A, with B's lane as its editor and C's Fill

The hand map is the overview and the assignment path (click a fingertip), the panel under it is one lane at 16 steps per bar for 1 to 8 bars, with templates and a Fill number from C. Chord fingers open a row of chords in the key instead of steps. Capture by tapping comes after the demo.
